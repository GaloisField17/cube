The White Cross

At the end of this step, we will have a white cross on the Up face, with each white edge matched to its corresponding center.

We start by rotating the cube so that the white center is on the Up face.

Many other beginner's methods start with the white center on the Down face. However, when starting out, it's often easier to work with the white center on the Up face as it is easier to see.

White Edge on the Up or Down Face

If a white edge is on the Up face, we first try to match it with its corresponding center by rotating the Up face.

If we cannot match the white edge without disturbing an already solved edge on the Up face, we first bring the white edge to the Down face by rotating the face it is on twice.

We then rotate the Down face to match it with its corresponding center.

Finally, we rotate the appropriate face twice to bring the edge to the Up face.

White Edge on the Middle Layer

In this case, we first try to bring the white edge to the Down face by rotating the face it is on.

We then match it with its corresponding center by rotating the Down face.

Finally, we bring the edge to the Up face by rotating the appropriate face twice.

If bringing the white edge down disturbs an already solved white edge on the Up face, we restore it at the end by performing the inverse rotation.

In this example, the white-green edge was disturbed by R. We therefore perform R' at the end to restore it.

White Edge on a Side Face

If the white edge is on one of the four side faces, we first bring it to the middle layer by rotating the face it is on.

We then rotate the Right or Left face to bring the edge to the Down face. From there, we match it with its corresponding center by rotating the Down face.

Finally, we bring the edge to the Up face by rotating the appropriate face.

If bringing the white edge down disturbs already solved white edges on the Up face, we restore them at the end by performing the inverse rotations.

In this example, two of the solved white edges were disturbed. First the white-orange edge by F', and then the white-green edge by R'. We therefore first perform R, then F at the end to restore them.

Note that for longer sequences, the order of corrections matters. To undo a sequence of disturbances, apply the corrections in reverse order, using the inverse of each move.

In the example on the right, the algorithm R F2 D L is applied to a solved cube. To restore the cube to its solved state, the corrections must be applied in reverse order as L' D' F2 R'.
