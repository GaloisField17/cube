At the end of this step, we will have completed the layer with the white center.

Depending on the orientation of the white facelet of the unsolved corner, we set up our moves as follows:

White Facelet on a Side Face

An unmatched white corner in the bottom layer with its white facelet facing neither the Up nor Down face presents the easiest case.

First, we rotate the Down face so that the corner's non-white facelet is aligned with its corresponding center.

We then rotate the cube so that the corner's white facelet is on the Front face.

We use R F' R' F, as shown in the upper example, or L' F L F', as shown in the lower example, to bring the corner onto the Up face.


Here, the corner is in the top layer with its white facelet facing a side face. We first rotate the appropriate face to bring the corner down. We then rotate the Down face to move the corner away. Finally, we perform the inverse rotation to restore the white cross. In this case, we use R' D' R.

When the corner is in the bottom layer with the white cross undisturbed on the Up face, we use the same procedure as in the previous case to bring the corner onto the Up face.

White Facelet Is Facing Up or Down

Using the procedure from the previous case, we can bring the white corner into the bottom layer without disturbing the white cross on the Up face.

If the mismatched corner's white facelet is facing up, we rotate the appropriate face to bring it into the bottom layer. In this case, we use R' D' R.

If the corner's white facelet is facing down, we rotate the appropriate face to move the corner into the bottom layer.

Once the corner is in the bottom layer and the white cross remains undisturbed on the Up face, we rotate the Down face until the corner's non-white facelet aligns with its corresponding center.

We then rotate the cube so that the corner's white facelet is on the Front face and use R F' R' F, as shown in the upper example, or L' F L F', as shown in the lower example, to bring the corner onto the Up face.
